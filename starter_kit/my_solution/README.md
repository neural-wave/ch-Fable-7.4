# Neuron migration: rules found by clustering, optimal trees and an exact scorer

## Approach

The rules are found by one generic pipeline, the same for every target. Cheap traditional machine learning finds
the candidate rules, a language model only refines them, and an exact scorer decides.

1. **Profiling.** Desbordante mines the keys (HyUCC) and the inclusion dependencies (Spider) of all legacy tables.
   From them the lab derives, without names and without an LLM:
   - the join graph, and which joins are temporal (into versioned tables);
   - the grain table of a target (n-ary inclusion of the target keys, reverse coverage);
   - the filter on its rows.
2. **Frame.** One row per target row, with every column reachable through the joins. A versioned table is read once
   per date of the grain row (`BR_TIER@PR_RCVDT` is the broker tier on the day the premium was received), and
   fixed-width codes are split by position (`RK_TARKEY[6]`).
3. **Clustering.** 1-D density clustering of target/input relations (ratio, difference, days). Every cluster center
   is a decimal constant. A row joins the center that reproduces it exactly with at least one rounding mode; rows
   that no center reproduces are noise, the candidate manual corrections.
4. **Optimal trees.** An exact dynamic program with the objective of GOSDT (fewest errors, then fewest leaves,
   depth at most 3) explains the cluster labels. It also finds the smallest feature sets that reach the optimum.
   CART is kept for comparison.
5. **Pool.** Every leaf becomes a partial rule, every tree a complete candidate. When the constants of the leaves
   are the values of a parameter table and the tree features are contained in its keys, the leaves generalize to a
   single lookup. Reference-date and rounding variants are added. Every candidate gets an exact score on a
   discovery split and a holdout split (one policy in five), and goes into a ledger.
6. **Language model, only where needed.** Claude Code, run headless (`claude -p`), reads a compact report and
   proposes at most three new features in a small, validated expression language (for example
   `at(BR_TIER, PR_RCVDT)`); each proposal is scored like every other candidate.
7. **Residuals.** The rows the best candidate gets wrong are clustered again. What no rule explains is noise, and
   goes to t5.

The winner is translated into code by deterministic templates (`lab freeze`), and `check.py` must give the same
score as the ledger.

## Rules found

### t0 commission

```
for each premium item of NRPREM with PR_STATE = "P":
    policy = the NRPOLH version valid on PR_RCVDT (highest version wins)
    rate   = NRCOMR.CR_RATE[CR_TIER = tier of the policy's broker (NRBROK), CR_PAYTP = 6th character of RK_TARKEY]
    amount = PR_AMTRC x rate, rounded half up to the cent
```

225,848 of 225,858 train rows are exact (holdout 44,051 of 44,052). The 10 other rows differ by whole currency
units and no rule reproduces them: they are manual corrections, 6 of them listed in NREXCP.

What decided the details:
- the broker tier on the money date beats the tier on the due date by 2 rows and the current tier by 1,430 rows;
- half-up rounding beats half-even on one row (BL10000869 #1, 399.205 -> 399.21).

Main commands: `lab run <task>`, `lab pool show <task>`, `lab feature <task> <name> <expr>`, `lab suggest <task>`,
`lab freeze <task>`, `lab score <task>`, `lab show <table> COL=VALUE`, `lab audit`. To run them, put the content of
`tools/` next to `legacy/`, `targets/`, `starter_kit/` and `SCHEMA.md` of the challenge folder, then
`cd lab && uv sync && uv run lab run t0_commission`; the Parquet files of the runs are rebuilt then.

The agent is Claude Code, started with `claude` in the root of the repository, or without supervision with
`scripts/claude-tasks.sh [task ...]` (one headless session per task). It follows the protocol in
`.claude/skills/rule-discovery/SKILL.md` (the skill `rule-discovery`). Step 5 of the lab (`lab suggest`) asks
Claude Code too, in headless mode (`claude -p`).

### Visualizations: `tools/lab/runs/atlas.html`

One offline page (`lab viz`) with three views. They read only the artifacts of the pipeline, so they work for a new
target too.

**1. Before / after: is a rule real?** A rule card (`lab ablate`) switches one rule off in a scratch copy of the
submission and scores it again; the submission itself never changes, so neither does its maintainability index. A
real rule breaks exactly the rows it explains and nothing else: for every card the page shows the wrong rows with
and without the rule, and where the broken rows are. Switching off the broker tier on the money date breaks 1,430
commissions, 1,422 of them received before the latest version of the policy began; Good Friday as a holiday breaks
158 collection dates, all on Good Friday 2027; the age at the last birthday instead of the nearest one breaks 1,053
projections, each with the next birthday within six months of the begin date; the system change breaks the due
dates of policies begun on the 29th to the 31st only. No card makes a single row right that the rule gets wrong. One
chart per task ranks the rules by the rows they make right (leave one out).

![Before / after of the tier on the money date](tools/docs/t6/rules.png)

**2. Policy timeline: where does a policy go wrong?** A subclass of the submission's `FundAccounting` replays one
policy with the submission's own code and writes down every booking. They are matched with NRBOOK and t3_booking,
the units with t3_holding, the values with t3_surrender_value, the projection of the offer with t2_projection_year,
and the rows of every task with what `lab score` computed. A red line marks the first value that differs: none for
BL10000001 (4,298 bookings, 285 values), 2025-12-31 for BL10000321, where every booking matches and only the
surrender value is off, by exactly 175.00 francs: a value typed by hand.

![Policy timeline of BL10000321](tools/docs/t6/policy.png)

**3. The journey of 100 francs.** A story in nine chapters for a reader who knows nothing about computers or
insurance, in English with an Italian switch. The policy is picked by the rules themselves: it crosses the system
change of 2007, pays monthly by direct debit, has a premium that a holiday moves, and every one of its values is exact.
Chapters 1-7 follow its money (due date, entry charge, commission, units, monthly charges, the two programs, the
annual statement and the surrender value); 8 and 9 tell how the rules were found and what the values typed by hand
are. A test keeps the words of the trade out and every term in bold in the glossary.

## Data quirks found by the audit

- NRCODE has 66 rows with a different number of fields than the header.
- NRFXRT has 80 lowercase currency codes.
- `PS_SMOKE` has the value `J` in 749 rows (documented: Y/N).
- NRPERS has 10 impossible birth dates, such as 31 September.
- The item states O, P, R and W are missing from NRCODE.
- NRFPRC has 664 prices published more than once.
- NRPOLH has 44 superseded versions with an open end.
- The batch system changed from CBLBATCH to NGBATCH on 2007-08-11.
